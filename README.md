# Is NeetCode Pro Worth It: What $119 a Year Actually Buys, Who Should Stick With the Free Roadmap, and the Text-First Alternative Worth Comparing

Most people typing this question into a search bar are already halfway to an answer. They've done the Blind 75 or part of it, they know the videos are free on YouTube, and they're trying to work out whether the paywall hides something they actually need or just a nicer dashboard.

So here's the direct version. NeetCode Pro is worth it for one specific reason — you want the same problems and explanations reorganized into course-shaped progression with AI help inside the editor, and you're going to use it for more than a month or two. For everyone else, the free roadmaps plus a structured text-based resource is a better use of the same money.

The rest of this breaks down what the paid tier includes, where the free tier already covers you, and the cheaper pattern-first option most "is it worth it" posts mention in one line and then skip past.

## What NeetCode Pro costs right now

NeetCode shows two paid plans on its Pro page. There's no monthly option listed, which surprises people who expect the usual $30-a-month subscription shape.

| Plan | Price shown | Terms | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Blind 75 and NeetCode 150 roadmaps, video explanations, NeetCode All list | Testing whether the teaching style clicks |
| One Year | $119 (listed against $199) | 12 months of access, auto-renew off by default, future content included during the access period | One focused prep cycle |
| Lifetime | $297 (listed against $599) | One-time payment, current and future content | People who expect to interview again in a few years |

Two details in that table matter more than the headline numbers.

First, auto-renew is off by default on the yearly plan. You pay $119, you get twelve months, and it stops. No surprise charge in month thirteen. If a subscription you forgot about is one of the reasons you're hesitating, that concern doesn't apply here.

Second, you'll find other prices quoted in older comparison posts — $149 a year, $219 lifetime, $175 a year in one write-up. Those figures don't match the current Pro page. Either the posts are stale or the pricing has moved around. Check the page before you buy rather than trusting a comparison table written a year ago.

## What $119 actually unlocks

The Pro page lists the paid features fairly plainly. Working through them:

- Video courses covering data structures, algorithms, and system design, organized as courses rather than a video list
- Practice problems you solve in the browser with a built-in editor, test cases, and progress tracking
- Written guides for people who'd rather read than watch
- Code solutions in eight languages — Python, Java, C++, JavaScript, C#, Go, Swift, and Kotlin
- Company tagged problems across 100+ companies, filterable so you can focus on your target employer
- NeetBot, a chat assistant inside the editor that answers questions and generates visual walkthroughs
- AI hints that escalate rather than dumping the answer, plus a debug tool that shows a side-by-side diff of your code against a suggested fix
- Cloud sync so your code follows you between devices
- Access to a private Discord

The AI features are the part that's genuinely new relative to what people were paying for two years ago. Graduated hints and a diff-based debugger are closer to what a decent study partner does than a static solution page.

There's one honest wrinkle. NeetCode's own pages quote different totals for the problem library — the pricing page says 1,000+ practice problems, the Pro page says 300+. Both numbers can't describe the same thing, and the company hasn't published which is which. Assume the Pro-specific set is in the low hundreds and the larger figure includes everything on the site. It doesn't change the buying decision much, but don't plan your prep around a specific count.

## Where the free tier already covers you

This is where most of the "worth it?" debate actually lives.

NeetCode's free material is not a teaser. The Blind 75 and NeetCode 150 roadmaps are open, and the video explanations attached to them are the same ones that made the brand. One review from August 2026 counted 824 problems accessible on the free side of the site. If your problem is "I don't know what to study," the free roadmaps solve it. If your problem is "I can't understand why this approach works," the free videos solve it too.

What free doesn't give you is the reorganized course structure, the progress tracking, the company filters, and the AI tooling. Free also leaves you solving problems on LeetCode with a NeetCode tab open, which is a workflow, not a flaw, but it is friction you're paying to remove.

Ask yourself one question: can I name the specific feature I'd lose by staying free? If your answer is "progress tracking and not having to think about what's next," that's a real need and $119 addresses it. If your answer is vague, you're buying reassurance.

## The lifetime math, done properly

$297 one-time against $119 a year breakeven at about two and a half years. Two yearly payments come to $238, which is less than lifetime. Three come to $357, which is more.

So lifetime only wins if you genuinely expect to be prepping again in the third year. A lot of engineers are — job changes every two to three years are normal, and the second prep cycle is usually faster than the first. Plenty aren't, and they should take the yearly plan and ignore the crossed-out $599. A discount against a price you were never going to pay isn't a saving.

## Who should not buy it

Three profiles where Pro is the wrong purchase right now:

**You've solved fewer than roughly a hundred problems.** The thing slowing you down at that stage is pattern recognition, not company tags. The free roadmaps cover the first part fine.

**You learn from reading, not watching.** NeetCode is video-first, and no amount of progress tracking changes that. If you consistently prefer text and diagrams, you'd be paying for the format you use least.

**You need system design for a senior loop.** NeetCode Pro includes system design content, but the platform's center of gravity is DSA. If you're interviewing at L5 and above, budget for dedicated system design prep separately rather than expecting the DSA subscription to stretch that far.

## A few questions people ask before buying

**Does NeetCode Pro replace LeetCode Premium?**
Not really. Pro gives you the teaching layer; LeetCode Premium gives you company-tagged questions sorted by frequency. If you're two weeks out from an onsite at a named company, the frequency data helps. Otherwise the tags inside Pro cover the same idea at a lower price.

**Is the one-year plan a subscription that renews?**
The page states auto-renew is off by default, so access ends when the twelve months do unless you turn it back on.

**What if I buy Pro and stop after a month?**
Then you've paid $119 for a month of use, which is the honest risk of a yearly plan with no monthly alternative. That risk is exactly why the free tier exists — use it properly for a couple of weeks first.

**Is AlgoMonster a straight downgrade because it's cheaper?**
No. It's a format choice. Text and diagrams suit some people better than video, and its curriculum covers patterns NeetCode teaches in a different order. The missing piece is video, and if you need video, that's decisive.

## The version of this answer that fits most people

If your prep window is one to three months, you're already comfortable with the free videos, and you want the roadmap to stop being something you maintain yourself, $119 for a year of NeetCode Pro is defensible. If you've seen the free content and can't articulate what's missing, you're not there yet — do another month of the free roadmap first.

And if the honest description of how you learn is "I read faster than I watch," then the question isn't whether NeetCode Pro is worth $119. It's whether you should be buying a video course at all.
